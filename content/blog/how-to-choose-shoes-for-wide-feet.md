"I have wide feet, should I just buy a size up?"

This is the most common question our support team receives. The short answer: absolutely not.

Sizing up solves the width issue but creates new problems: the shoe is too long, your heel slips, and the arch support sits in the wrong place — leading to plantar fasciitis. To solve this, you need to understand the foundation of shoemaking: the shoe last.

## The Problem with Standard Western Lasts

The shoe last is the 3D mold that dictates the internal volume of the shoe. Most global brands use standard lasts based on Western foot shapes. However, ergonomic data shows distinct differences in Asian foot morphology:

- **Wider forefoot:** greater ball girth.
- **Higher instep:** thicker midfoot volume.
- **Flatter heel:** different Achilles curvature.

If you have a typical Asian wide-foot / high-instep profile, wearing a standard Western last will cause pinky-toe compression and lace bite, even if the length is correct.

## 3 Rules for Buying Wide-Fit Shoes

1. **Look for specific widths (2E / wide).** Don't just look for a wider sole — the entire upper volume must be adjusted.
2. **Check the toe-box shape.** Avoid almond or pointed toes. Opt for rounded or squared toe boxes with adequate vertical height.
3. **Material adaptability.** Rigid leather offers zero forgiveness. Materials like flyknit or elastomeric 3D printed lattices adapt to your foot's temperature and pressure, effectively "memorizing" your unique shape.
