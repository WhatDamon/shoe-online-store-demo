Customers often ask: "If your shoes are selling well, why don't you stock up? I sometimes have to wait a few days for my size."

It's a fair question. But today, I want to explain why we intentionally stick to a small-batch, on-demand production model.

## The Hidden Cost of Deadstock

In traditional footwear manufacturing, inventory is the root of all evil. Brands over-order to get lower factory prices, resulting in warehouses full of unsold shoes. To clear them, they discount, which devalues the brand and creates a vicious cycle. Worse, millions of unsold shoes end up in landfills or incinerators every year.

As a startup, we refuse to play this wasteful game. More importantly, we refuse to pass the cost of waste onto our consumers.

## The Agility of Additive Manufacturing

3D printing gives us unprecedented flexibility. We base our weekly print schedules on real-time website data.

Yes, this means popular sizes might show a "3-5 day lead time." But those few days guarantee zero deadstock and zero resource waste. The money we save on warehousing and markdowns is reinvested directly into R&D and better post-processing materials.

## Additive vs. Subtractive Waste

From a purely environmental standpoint, 3D printing (additive) only uses the material needed to build the part. Unlike traditional cutting (subtractive), there are no off-cuts or scrap margins.

When you see "Ships in 5 Days" on our site, don't be frustrated. It means your shoes are currently being printed and hand-finished in our Wenzhou workshop, specifically for you. That is our most honest definition of sustainable fashion.
